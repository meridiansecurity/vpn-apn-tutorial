Orientation · Section 2

Background & Scenario

Remote access is necessary for Meridian's work, but it also moves important security decisions outside the physical office perimeter.

The company and the two access paths

Meridian Defense Systems is a hypothetical prime contractor of roughly 2,000 employees supporting Department of Defense programs involving CUI. About 600 engineers work remotely through a commercial VPN appliance. Another 200 field personnel use government-furnished mobile devices that reach corporate resources through a carrier-provided Access Point Name (APN).

Plain-language definitions

VPN (Virtual Private Network): an encrypted connection that allows an authorized remote user to reach internal resources across networks the company does not control.

APN (Access Point Name): part of the cellular network architecture that determines how a mobile device connects to a packet data network. A private APN can give an organization more control over how managed mobile traffic reaches company resources.

Both technologies create remote-access risk when configurations are not reviewed regularly. The VPN is particularly exposed because its gateway must accept remote connections, while the APN depends on carrier-managed infrastructure and proper mobile-device enrollment.

Why management should care

  • Program managers can lose schedule when systems are taken offline for investigation and recovery.
  • The contracts organization carries compliance and eligibility risk when required assessments or affirmations lapse.
  • Government program offices depend on Meridian to protect information held on their behalf.
  • Mission users ultimately depend on the availability and confidentiality of the systems these programs support.

How the environment developed

  1. 1990s IPsec develops through the IETF

    IPsec becomes a widely used method for protecting traffic across untrusted networks.

  2. 2016 DFARS safeguarding requirements

    DFARS 252.204-7012 establishes contractual requirements for safeguarding covered defense information and reporting cyber incidents.

  3. 2020 VPN and Zero Trust guidance

    NIST publishes SP 800-77 Rev. 1 and SP 800-207 as remote work expands and VPN use grows.

  4. 2024 CMMC program rule

    The CMMC program rule takes effect under 32 C.F.R. Part 170.

  5. Nov 2025 CMMC enters contract implementation

    DFARS 252.204-7021 establishes CMMC requirements for applicable covered contracts.

  6. Jul 2026 CMMC Phase 2 suspended for review

    The Department of Defense pauses the upcoming third-party certification phase while a reform review is conducted.

Management decision

The CMMC pause changes Meridian's immediate compliance timeline, but it does not reduce the underlying security risk. The additional time gives management an opportunity to address known gaps while the certification requirements are being reviewed.

Domain 1 of 4 · Section 3

Communications & Networking Security

Recent exploitation of internet-facing network infrastructure shows why remote-access systems need timely patching, configuration review, and clear ownership.

What recent activity shows

Recent CISA activity shows that internet-facing network infrastructure continues to be actively targeted and requires timely patching and review (CISA, 2026a).

In June 2026, Check Point disclosed CVE-2026-50751, a critical VPN vulnerability rated 9.3 that could allow an unauthenticated attacker to establish a remote-access session without valid credentials. CISA added the vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog shortly after disclosure. Exploitation had already been occurring, and at least one incident involved a Qilin ransomware affiliate (Rapid7, 2026).

A typical attack path

This simplified sequence helps explain why vulnerability remediation is ranked first in Meridian's action plan.

  1. The remote-access service is exposed. The VPN gateway has to accept connections from users outside the company's physical network.
  2. A vulnerability becomes known. Public details and exploit techniques can spread quickly after disclosure.
  3. Automated scanning finds exposed systems. An attacker does not need to specifically target Meridian before discovering an unpatched device.
  4. The attacker attempts to establish access. If authentication or the appliance itself is compromised, the attacker can move past the remote-access boundary.
Research finding

Qollakaj et al. (2025) found that misconfiguration, weak authentication, and implementation problems are major contributors to VPN risk. For Meridian, that supports fixing patching and configuration problems before jumping immediately to a major architectural replacement.

Domain 2 of 4 · Section 4

Access & Identity Management

Remote access is only as trustworthy as the identity controls behind it. A stolen reusable credential can look legitimate unless stronger authentication is required.

The identity problem

A VPN relying on passwords or reusable pre-shared credentials may accept a stolen credential as though it belongs to the legitimate user. That is why remote-access security cannot depend on a password alone.

What the Check Point example shows

Check Point's mitigation for CVE-2026-50751 included making machine-certificate authentication mandatory for affected deployments. Stronger certificate-based authentication helps, but it does not replace the need to keep the VPN appliance patched.

Plain-language definition

Phishing-resistant MFA: authentication designed so that stealing a password or tricking a user into entering a one-time code is not enough to gain access. PIV smart cards and certificate-based authentication are examples that fit Meridian's defense environment.

The mobile side of identity

Meridian's APN creates a different access problem. A private APN can provide more control over how mobile traffic is routed, but that benefit depends on the device being properly enrolled and the APN configuration being enforced.

NIST (2023) recommends managing enterprise mobile-device connections through centralized controls. For Meridian, that means MDM enrollment and private APN access should operate together rather than as unrelated safeguards.

What this means for Meridian

  • Retire password-only and reusable pre-shared remote-access authentication where practical.
  • Use certificate- and PIV-based authentication with phishing-resistant MFA.
  • Require managed-device enrollment before government-furnished mobile devices can use the private APN.

Domain 3 of 4 · Section 5

Security Operations

Preventing every intrusion is unrealistic. Meridian also needs enough visibility to recognize suspicious VPN and APN activity before a small problem becomes a major incident.

The detection gap

247 days
Average time to identify and contain a breach (IBM, 2026)
$11.5M
Average breach cost at a U.S. organization reported by IBM (2026)

IBM (2026) reported that organizations averaged 247 days to identify and contain a breach, and incidents with longer lifecycles were considerably more expensive than those resolved sooner. The management point is straightforward: detection speed affects both operational disruption and financial impact.

Where Meridian's early-warning data exists

VPN and APN session information can show who connected, when the connection occurred, and what remote-access path was used. If that data stays only on individual appliances or carrier systems, it is much harder to correlate activity and recognize suspicious patterns.

Recommended operational change

Centralize VPN and APN session logging and add anomaly alerting. This gives Meridian a better chance of detecting abnormal remote-access behavior before an incident remains hidden for months.

Domain 4 of 4 · Section 6

Laws, Regulations & Compliance

The CMMC Phase 2 suspension changes Meridian's regulatory timeline, but it does not remove the underlying obligations that apply to contracts involving CUI.

The July 2026 pause

On July 13, 2026, the Department of Defense suspended CMMC Phase 2, freezing upcoming third-party certification requirements while a 60-day reform review was conducted (DefenseScoop, 2026).

The pause does not mean Meridian can treat cybersecurity requirements as optional. For applicable CUI-covered contracts, requirements tied to DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2, SPRS scoring, and annual affirmation remain important management responsibilities.

Three categories management should separate

Meridian compliance requirements grouped by current status
Status What falls in it Management takeaway
Binding today DFARS 252.204-7012; NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 implementation; current SPRS score; annual affirmation These obligations remain relevant to Meridian's applicable CUI-covered contracts.
Suspended Third-party CMMC Level 2 certification under the paused Phase 2 rollout The requirement is suspended rather than permanently eliminated, so management should continue preparing.
Authoritative guidance NIST SP 800-77 Rev. 1; SP 800-124 Rev. 2; SP 800-207 These publications provide useful technical and architectural guidance even when they are not independent contract requirements.

Conditional versus final CMMC Level 2

Management and budget issue

Final Level 2 status requires all 110 applicable Level 2 security requirements to receive a MET finding. Conditional Level 2 can be achieved with a minimum score of 88 only when the remaining deficiencies are eligible for a Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) under 32 C.F.R. § 170.21(a)(2)(iii). Conditional status begins a 180-day remediation period. Management should not budget or plan around a score of 88 as though it represents final compliance.

Why these frameworks

ISO/IEC 27001, COBIT, and the CIS Controls all offer useful cybersecurity or governance guidance, but they are not as directly connected to the requirements in Meridian's DoD contracting environment.

NIST CSF 2.0 was selected for governance because its Govern function connects cybersecurity decisions to organizational risk, responsibilities, policy, and oversight. NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 provides the risk methodology used in this tutorial, while the applicable CMMC and SP 800-171 requirements provide the contractual baseline.

Assessment · Section 7

Risk Assessment

Seven remote-access risks were evaluated using a qualitative likelihood-by-impact approach based on NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1.

Methodology

Likelihood reflects observed frequency in federal breach and directive reporting over the twelve months preceding the capstone. Impact reflects the combined operational, contractual, and financial consequence to Meridian. The final rating is based on the intersection of those two factors.

The ratings are qualitative and apply only to the hypothetical Meridian environment. They should be reassessed after material changes to the architecture or threat environment.

Why Recommendation 1 is risk-tiered

In June 2026, CISA issued Binding Operational Directive 26-04, replacing its previous uniform KEV remediation approach with a risk-based model that considers factors such as asset exposure, active exploitation, exploitability, and potential impact (CISA, 2026b).

The directive applies to federal civilian agencies rather than Meridian, but its risk factors provide a useful model for prioritizing remediation based on actual exposure rather than treating every vulnerability the same way.

Management takeaway

Most of the highest-ranked actions are basic security practices rather than major technology replacements. Patching, stronger authentication, split-tunneling controls, logging, and compliance review can reduce substantial risk before Meridian begins a larger Zero Trust transition.

Cost perspective

The moderate investments in this plan still represent a small fraction of the $11.5 million average breach cost reported for U.S. organizations by IBM (2026).

Final management takeaway

Meridian does not need to replace every part of its remote-access architecture at once. The most useful near-term approach is to address known vulnerabilities, strengthen identity controls, restrict traffic appropriately, centralize monitoring, maintain compliance visibility, and then move toward Zero Trust in phases.
