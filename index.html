Orientation · Section 2

Background & Scenario

Who Meridian is, why its remote-access architecture deserves executive attention rather than delegation, and how a decade of technology and regulation led here.

The company and the two paths

Meridian Defense Systems is a prime contractor of roughly 2,000 employees supporting Department of Defense programs involving Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI). About 600 engineers work remotely through a commercial VPN appliance, and 200 field personnel carry government-furnished devices that reach the corporate network through a carrier-provided Access Point Name (APN). Between them, those two paths carry nearly all of the company's sensitive traffic — and neither runs through a building Meridian owns.

Plain-language definitions

VPN (Virtual Private Network): an encrypted tunnel that lets a remote employee reach internal systems as if they were in the office. The gateway must be reachable from the open internet to work — which means attackers can reach it too.

APN (Access Point Name): the gateway a mobile device uses to move from the cellular carrier's network into a company's network. It must accept connections from devices moving through territory the company does not control.

Both systems are exposed by design, and both tend to be configured once during deployment and inherited unreviewed thereafter. This arrangement is unremarkable across the defense industrial base — which is exactly why it deserves executive attention.

Who has a stake

  • Program managers lose schedule when systems go offline for forensics.
  • The contracts organization carries eligibility risk: a reportable incident or lapsed affirmation affects award standing.
  • Government program offices depend on the confidentiality of information Meridian holds on their behalf.
  • The warfighter relying on these programs bears the operational consequence.

How we got here

  1. 1990sIPsec standardized

    Developed through the IETF, IPsec becomes the standard mechanism for carrying protected traffic across untrusted networks.

  2. 2016DFARS 252.204-7012 takes effect

    Safeguarding covered defense information and cyber-incident reporting become contract conditions.

  3. 2020NIST consolidates guidance; remote work surges

    SP 800-77 Rev. 1 (IPsec VPNs) and SP 800-207 (Zero Trust Architecture) publish as the pandemic drives a sustained rise in VPN-targeted attacks.

  4. 2024CMMC program rule finalized

    The CMMC program takes effect under 32 CFR Part 170.

  5. Nov 2025CMMC becomes a condition of award

    DFARS 252.204-7021 makes CMMC certification a condition of award for covered contracts.

  6. Jul 13, 2026CMMC Phase 2 suspended

    The Department of War freezes third-party certification requirements scheduled for November 2026, pending a reform review.

The management decision

The compliance ceiling dropped in July 2026. The threat did not. Meridian can read the CMMC pause as permission to slow down — or as an opportunity to close gaps on its own schedule. This tutorial recommends the second option.

Domain 1 of 4 · Section 3

Communications & Networking Security

Internet-facing network gear was the most actively attacked category of infrastructure across the federal landscape in the past year. Two 2026 campaigns show exactly how it happens.

What happened in 2026

In February 2026, CISA issued Emergency Directive 26-03, requiring federal agencies to inventory, patch, and assess for compromise across Cisco Software-Defined Wide Area Network (SD-WAN) systems after observing active exploitation. Four related vulnerabilities entered the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog between February and May (CISA, 2026a).

In June, Check Point disclosed CVE-2026-50751, a CVSS 9.3 flaw in certificate validation during IKEv1 key exchange that allowed an unauthenticated attacker to establish a remote-access VPN session without valid credentials. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on June 8. Exploitation had been underway since early May, and at least one incident involved a Qilin ransomware affiliate (Rapid7, 2026).

The mechanism, step by step

Understanding this chain is what justifies the patching recommendation later in this tutorial:

  1. The VPN concentrator listens on a public address.It has to — that is how remote employees reach it.
  2. A vulnerability is disclosed.Proof-of-concept exploit code circulates within days.
  3. Automated scanning locates every unpatched instance.Meridian's included — without anyone having targeted the company specifically.
  4. The attacker establishes a session as a trusted remote user.Further post-authentication activity is required to reach internal resources, but the perimeter has been crossed without a credential.
The encouraging finding

A systematic review of the post-pandemic VPN landscape concluded that the primary weaknesses are weak configuration and implementation, not inherent protocol flaws (Qollakaj et al., 2025). That argues for disciplined configuration and patch governance ahead of expensive architectural replacement.

Domain 2 of 4 · Section 4

Access & Identity Management

A VPN that authenticates with a password treats a stolen password as a legitimate employee. Identity is where most intrusions actually begin.

The problem

Phishing remained the most common initial access vector for the fourth consecutive year (IBM, 2026). A VPN authenticating by password or pre-shared key has no way to distinguish the real employee from an attacker who harvested that credential through a phishing page. To the appliance, both sessions are identical.

What the vendors themselves concluded

Check Point's own mitigation for CVE-2026-50751 is instructive: the fix was to make machine-certificate authentication mandatory, because the flaw affected deployments that did not require it. The vendor's answer was more certificate enforcement, not less — though the device enforcing authentication still has to be patched, which is why Domains 1 and 2 work together.

Plain-language definition

Phishing-resistant MFA: multi-factor authentication that cannot be satisfied by tricking a user into typing a code into a fake page — for example, hardware-bound certificates or PIV smart cards. A push notification or SMS code can be phished; a certificate bound to a managed device cannot be typed into anything.

What this means for Meridian

  • Retire pre-shared keys and password-only VPN authentication.
  • Move to certificate- and PIV-based authentication with phishing-resistant MFA (Recommendation 2 in this tutorial).
  • Treat identity as the control that keeps a phished employee from becoming a network intrusion.

Domain 3 of 4 · Section 5

Security Operations

The average breach goes 247 days before it is identified and contained. The data that would catch it earlier already exists at Meridian — it is just sitting in the wrong place.

Time is the multiplier. Incidents that run past 200 days cost, on average, well over a million dollars more than those caught sooner — before considering the U.S. average breach cost of $11.5 million (IBM, 2026).

Where Meridian's early warning already lives

VPN and APN session data — who connected, from where, when, and how much data moved — would surface an anomaly early. Yet this data is routinely left on the appliance itself rather than centralized where analysts and alerting tools can see it. An attacker who compromises the appliance can then also tamper with the only copy of the evidence.

The operational change

Centralize VPN and APN session logging and add anomaly alerting (Recommendation 4). This is a moderate investment that directly attacks the 247-day problem: you cannot respond to what you cannot see.

Domain 4 of 4 · Section 6

Laws, Regulations & Compliance

The CMMC Phase 2 suspension changed Meridian's regulatory calendar — not its obligations. Sorting what is binding, what is paused, and what is voluntary prevents both overspending and false comfort.

The July 2026 pause

On July 13, 2026, the Department of War suspended CMMC Phase 2, freezing third-party certification requirements scheduled for contracts on November 10, 2026 (DefenseScoop, 2026). Department CIO Kirsten Davies cited Small Business Administration estimates that later CMMC phases could cost small and mid-sized firms more than $7 billion annually, with over 100,000 companies requiring assessment from roughly 100 authorized organizations. The pause is pending a Reform Task Force review due in mid-September 2026.

Critically, the suspension came by memorandum, not regulation — it is restorable as quickly as it was withdrawn (Eyre & Zhou, 2026).

The SPRS scoring rule that catches budgets off guard

Frequently misstated — with budget consequences

Final CMMC Level 2 status requires a MET result on all 110 requirements — a perfect SPRS score of 110. A score of 88 or above earns only Conditional Level 2 status, and only when every unmet requirement is documented in a Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) and none appears among the requirements that cannot be deferred under 32 CFR 170.21(a)(2)(iii). Conditional status starts a 180-day clock; failure to close the POA&M causes the status to expire. Budgeting to a score of 88 as though it were compliance would be a mistake.

Why these frameworks and not others

ISO/IEC 27001 offers a mature management system but carries no weight with a DoD contracting officer. COBIT addresses network security too generally to drive configuration decisions. The CIS Controls are practical but not referenced in DFARS clauses. NIST CSF 2.0 was selected for governance because its Govern function locates risk decisions with senior leadership rather than the IT department (NIST, 2024), and SP 800-30 Rev. 1 supplies the risk methodology used in the next section. CMMC and its SP 800-171 baseline are not optional for a defense contractor.

Assessment · Section 7

Risk Assessment

Seven risks, scored with the qualitative likelihood-by-impact approach in NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. Select any risk on the heat map to highlight its entry in the register below.

Methodology in one paragraph

Likelihood reflects observed frequency in federal breach and directive reporting over the past twelve months. Impact reflects the combined operational, contractual, and financial consequence to Meridian. The composite rating is the intersection of the two on a three-by-three matrix (NIST, 2012). Ratings should be re-scored after any material change to the network architecture.

Why a tiered patching standard (Recommendation 1)

On June 10, 2026, CISA issued Binding Operational Directive 26-04, superseding BOD 22-01 and replacing its flat KEV deadline with a tiered model that scores each vulnerability against asset exposure, KEV status, exploit automation, and post-exploitation impact — producing remediation windows from three days to the next scheduled upgrade (CISA, 2026b). Such directives bind federal civilian agencies, not the defense industrial base. But it is the benchmark assessors and insurers will reference, and a flat rule is exactly the undifferentiated approach CISA has moved away from.

The cost argument

The moderate investments above carry real expense — and remain a small fraction of the $11.5 million average breach cost at a U.S. organization (IBM, 2026). Recommendations 1–3 are largely policy and configuration, not capital projects.
